    How it works

    One trading decision a day, made by a program.

    What to expect

    Together the account aims for about 19% a year of ups and downs (assuming the two parts move largely independently). That means bigger expected gains than either part alone, and falls of 25–35% are realistic at some point. Roughly one year in three may do worse than cash, and losing stretches can last several years. Paper trading also flatters results a little, because simulated orders don't move prices. Past performance is not a guide to the future.

    The two parts

    World shares fund. Over long periods, shares worldwide have usually done better than cash, but with deep falls along the way (more than 50% in 2008–09). Up to 85% of the account goes into one global index fund, sized so this part swings by about 10% a year, and bought in steps of up to £250,000 a day.

    Futures across 27 markets. Share indices, government bonds, currencies, metals, energy and farm goods. A future is a contract to buy or sell later at a price fixed now. It needs only a deposit, so a small amount of money controls a much larger face value. For each market the system follows the price trend (markets that have been rising tend to keep rising for a while, and the same for falling) and leans towards markets that pay you to hold them. It can bet on prices falling as well as rising, which has tended to help during long, drawn-out market falls; it does not protect the part held in the world fund. This part aims for about 16% a year of ups and downs.

    How decisions are made

    From 8 October, one decision a day, using the previous day's closing prices, traded at about 16:05 UK time. Trading more often would mostly add costs: the published evidence says the profits in these strategies come from slow, steady positioning. Day 1 had two set-up runs in the morning (the first used the price trend only, the second added the pays-to-hold part), so some positions were reversed within minutes.

    New trading strategies are tested on years of history against pass marks fixed in advance; two stock-picking strategies failed that test on day 1 and were dropped. The world fund and the higher risk level were judgement calls based on long-run published evidence, not tested strategies.

    Safety

    The program can only reach the paper account. A market whose data is missing or suspect is left untouched (only contracts about to expire are still moved to the next month), and if more than three are affected nothing is rebalanced that day. Every position has a size cap, and a stop switch blocks all new orders.

    What the words mean
    Account value (NAV)
    What the whole account is worth: cash plus everything held.
    Long and Short
    Long is a bet on the price rising; Short is a bet on it falling. The account gains if the price moves the way of the bet and loses if it moves the other way.
    Face value (notional)
    The full value of the contracts in pounds. Futures need only a deposit, so face values can add up to more than the account.
    % of account
    Face value divided by the account value.
    Open profit/loss (unrealised)
    Gains or losses on holdings that are still open, so not yet banked.
    Day change
    How much a holding, or the whole account, has moved since the last closing value.
    Closing value
    The account's value recorded at 21:35 UK time each weekday.
    Catch-up reading
    A value recorded the next morning when the 21:35 closing value was missed. It stands in for the missed close.
    Typical yearly swing
    Roughly how much a holding might gain or lose in an ordinary year. Bad years can be much bigger, and it is not the most that could be lost.
    Share of the risk: on its own and after offsets
    On its own counts each holding as if nothing else were held. After offsets allows for holdings that tend to move together or against each other over the past year, so a holding can count for more, less, or even below zero.
    Signal (forecast, scaled −20 to +20)
    How strongly the system leans towards rising (+) or falling (−) prices. The size of a holding follows its signal.
    Price trend (EWMAC)
    Whether the price has been rising or falling over recent weeks and months.
    Pays to hold (carry)
    Whether holding the contract earns or costs money while prices stay still.
    Average price (incl. costs)
    The average price of a holding, including commission, in the market's own price units.
    Contract month
    When a futures contract ends. Holdings are moved to the next month before then.
    Set-up run
    The first-day runs that built the starting positions.
    Late daily run
    A daily run that started after 17:00 UK time, for example because the computer was off at 16:05.
    Held back
    An order that was not sent because its market had closed. It goes at the next daily run.
    Typical yearly ups and downs (volatility)
    How much the daily value moves, scaled up to a year.
    Fall from a high (drawdown)
    How far the value is below the highest value it had reached before.
    Moves with the world fund (correlation)
    From −1 to +1: how closely daily moves follow the world fund's daily moves.
    Return per unit of ups and downs (Sharpe ratio)
    The yearly return above cash, divided by the yearly ups and downs.
    Cash rate (SONIA)
    The Bank of England's overnight interest rate, used for the cash comparison.
    Diversification multipliers (IDM, FDM)
    Factors that scale holdings up because markets and signal parts do not all move together.

    How the figures are worked out
    Account value
    Cash plus the value of everything held, in pounds, as reported by the broker at each reading.
    Day change
    The account value now minus the last closing value before today's date.
    Open profit/loss
    The sum of each holding's gain or loss since it was opened, in pounds.
    Since the start
    The account value minus the £1,000,000 it started with.
    World fund instead and Cash instead
    £1,000,000 grown in line with the world fund's price, or with the Bank of England overnight rate, from the same day.
    Face value and % of account
    Contracts held times contract size times price, in pounds; divided by the account value for the %.
    Average and last price
    In each market's own quoted units (for example US cents for corn), so they match the price chart and the orders.
    Day change of a holding
    Its value at the latest snapshot, minus its value at the previous snapshot, minus the cost of any trades in between and their commission, in pounds.
    Everything else
    The account's change minus the holdings' total: bets already closed, fees, interest and currency effects.
    Share of the risk on its own
    A holding's typical yearly swing divided by the sum of all holdings' swings.
    Share of the risk after offsets
    Each holding's part of the whole account's swing, from the past year's daily profit and loss of today's holdings; the parts add up to 100%.
    Whole-account yearly swing
    The spread of the past year's daily profit and loss of today's holdings, taken together and scaled to a year.
    A bad day and the worst day
    The day that 1 day in 20 was worse than, and the single worst day, in the past year's daily profit and loss of today's holdings.
    Growth chart
    Each line is its value divided by its value at the start of the selected range, minus 1.
    Fall from the highest value
    The value divided by the highest value so far in the range, minus 1.
    Yearly rate
    (End value ÷ start value) to the power of (252 ÷ trading days), minus 1.
    Ups and downs
    The spread (standard deviation) of daily returns times the square root of 252.
    Return per unit of ups and downs
    The average daily return above cash times 252, divided by the spread of those daily returns times the square root of 252.
    Share of days up, biggest daily rise and fall
    Counted over the daily returns in the selected range. An intraday reading taken today is left out until its day closes.
    Longest spell below a previous high
    The longest run of trading days in a row on which the value was below its highest value so far.
    Monthly returns
    Each month's last value divided by the previous month's last value, minus 1; the first month starts from £1,000,000.
    Profit/loss by asset class
    Still open is the holdings' open profit/loss; the total comes from every recorded fill since the start at today's exchange rates, so closed amounts are approximate.
    Signal
    The price trend part and the pays-to-hold part combined, then capped at −20 and +20.
